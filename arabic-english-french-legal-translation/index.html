The real difficulty

Legal terms rarely map one to one.

Three languages, three legal traditions. A word-for-word translation can mislead. Here are examples of what we watch for.

01

Notary

A notary public in the UAE (كاتب العدل) and a French notaire are not the same office. The notaire is a legal professional who drafts and authenticates deeds, so rendering one as the other can mislead a reader about who did what.

02

Consideration

English contract law relies on the idea of consideration. Civil-law drafting in Arabic and French builds the same bargain on different concepts, so the term has no single word to carry across. We express the substance and note the source term where it matters.

03

Defined terms

Contracts define a term once and use it throughout. We keep one glossary per project, so a defined term is rendered identically in every clause and every language.

One team

Consistent terminology across the whole file.

When a tender, a due-diligence bundle or a long case file spans several documents and two or three languages, the risk is drift: the same term rendered three ways. Using one team for every direction means one glossary, one reviewer and one set of decisions, applied from the first document to the last.

This is especially useful for companies with contracts in more than one of the three languages, law firms on cross-border matters, and organisations responding to tenders. For large multi-document work, see our bulk and tender service on the main services list.
