Two directions

Where is the document going?

The steps and their order depend on where the document was issued and where it will be used. Find your case below.

Issued in the UAE, used abroad

For a UAE document that a foreign authority, employer or university has asked for.

  1. Issuing authority in the UAE issues the document.
  2. Issuing-body attestation, if required. For example, the Ministry of Education for educational certificates.
  3. UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) attests the document.
  4. Embassy of the destination country, if that country requires it.

Issued abroad, used in the UAE

For a foreign document that a UAE authority, employer or court has asked for.

  1. Issuing body in the country of issue.
  2. Foreign ministry of the issuing country attests it.
  3. UAE embassy in that country attests it.
  4. UAE MOFA attests it after the document arrives in the UAE.
  5. Certified legal translation into Arabic if the document is in another language.
  6. Attestation of the translation, only if the receiving body requires it.
The golden rule. Issuing body, then the foreign ministry of the issuing country, then the UAE embassy, then UAE MOFA, then certified legal translation if needed. Skipping or reordering a step can mean the document is refused.
At a glance

The route for a foreign document used in the UAE.

Issuing body→ Foreign ministry→ UAE embassy→ UAE MOFA→ Certified legal translation

Example. An Egyptian university certificate used in the UAE goes from the university to the Egyptian Ministry of Higher Education, then to the Egyptian foreign ministry, then to the UAE embassy in Cairo, then to UAE MOFA, and finally to certified legal translation if the receiving body needs it in Arabic.

FAQ

Document attestation in the UAE: common questions.

What is the correct order for attesting a foreign document for use in the UAE?+

Issuing body, then the foreign ministry of the issuing country, then the UAE embassy in that country, then UAE MOFA, then certified legal translation into Arabic if the document is in another language. Notarisation and attestation of the original come before the translation.

Does an apostille replace the embassy and MOFA steps?+

No. The UAE is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, so an apostille on its own is not accepted for use in the UAE. Documents still go through the foreign ministry, the UAE embassy and UAE MOFA.

Do I translate the document before or after attestation?+

In the usual route the original is attested first and the certified translation is prepared afterwards. If the receiving body asks for a different order, follow its instruction and tell us.

Does the translation also need to be attested?+

Only if the receiving body requires it. Ask the authority that will receive the document, and tell us so we can plan the order.

What about UAE documents used abroad?+

The usual route is the issuing authority, then issuing-body attestation if required (such as the Ministry of Education for certificates), then UAE MOFA, then the embassy of the destination country if it requires one.
